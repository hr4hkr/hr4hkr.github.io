From line items to business questions

I checked missing product and customer fields, then created sales value as quantity multiplied by unit price. Date fields were expanded into month, weekday, year, and hour so the transaction file could be summarized across time, countries, products, and customers.

The exploratory views include distributions of quantity and price, monthly and weekday sales, country-level sales, top products and customers, and a simple count of product pairs within invoices.

What this view can—and cannot—say

Country and customer summaries help identify where the recorded sales are concentrated; product and time summaries can guide follow-up questions about assortment and trading patterns. They are descriptive results, not evidence that a marketing change would cause revenue to grow.

Cleaning note. The notebook removes negative-quantity rows before it checks cancellation invoices, so the reported “zero cancellations” is not a valid count. The revenue total is for the rows retained after filtering, not net sales after verified returns. Pair counts are also a simple co-occurrence tally; they should be deduplicated per invoice before being treated as a robust basket analysis.

Next steps

Separate cancellation and return records before filtering, label the revenue metric as gross or net, calculate customer and product rates with clear denominators, and use unique products per invoice for basket comparisons.
